Judge the asked relation between the named subject and target in the single original photograph. Keep this order throughout and submit A=yes or B=no.

Locate the two physical instances using the scene and question. Distinguish reflections, parts and overlapping instances. Keep each object's identity consistent throughout the judgment. Detecting a category or shifting a box center alone does not establish its relation.

Judge the exact predicate in ordinary scene context. Spatial relations can coexist: an object can be beside another and also partly in front of it. Compare overall placement and relevant extents for left/right and above/below; projected centers are unreliable for nested or strongly overlapping objects. For over/under, inspect covering and vertical alignment as well as position. For near/far, use scene scale, perspective and intervening space.

For on/on top of, inspect the support arrangement, including a thin covering or packaging between an object and its support. For touching, connected, detached or against, inspect contact or a gap. For inside/contains/outside/surrounding and part-whole, inspect boundaries and structural membership. For facing and axis relations, use the visible head, face, front surface or axis; reaching toward something or foot placement alone is weak evidence when the relevant orientation is hidden.

Use a direct JSON answer when vision and existing observations suffice. Request a tool only for a specific unresolved measurement. For front/behind, predicted depth can supplement occlusion and perspective. Select representative visible interior surface pixels in the original image, avoiding background, boundaries and occluders; check original width/height before calling. Larger point_depth data['depth'] means farther from the camera in this view. Extended objects may span several depths, so one point is not the whole object.

After tool calls, yield successful result IDs and inspect their actual values in the next request before deciding. State how the observed ordering supports the question; when it disagrees with an earlier impression, reconsider object identity and sampled surfaces using the photograph. Depth is predicted per-view camera evidence, not metric distance or certified object geometry. Detection boxes and category-confirmed masks do not certify support, contact or containment.
